# LBC-023 · Mutable IPNS C2 recovery

Evidence: **Observed** · Reviewed 2026-10-15
Chains: IPFS, IPNS · Networks: Not applicable

A signed mutable name supplies replacement C2 configuration through distributed content.

## Analysis procedure

### Collect

Stable IPNS name, fallback trigger, time-stamped resolution and resulting CID/path.

### Inspect

Preserve the IPNS record where available, verification status and the exact target fetched at that time.

### Correlate

Correlate the failed original connection, IPNS recovery and new downstream request.

### Interpretation limits

IPNS names are mutable names, not CIDs. A present-day resolution cannot establish an old C2 target.

Methods: [IPFS, IPNS and Arweave](content.md).

## Catalog context

Zscaler documents TransferLoader retrieving a replacement C2 through an IPNS name after connection failure. Volexity shows an IPNS route and gateway fallback in POWERSTAR. The IPNS name can remain stable while its target changes; it is not itself a CID.

Examples: TransferLoader; POWERSTAR

### Detection guidance

Capture /ipns/ names, resolution times and resolved CIDs separately. Correlate a prior connection failure, gateway lookup and new HTTPS or raw TCP destination.

### Defensive approach

Investigate or restrict the specific IPNS name where supported. Retain historical targets; one blocked old CID may not cover a mutable pointer.

## ATT&CK assessment

Enterprise · Technique · Reported behavior

TransferLoader uses an IPNS resource to recover replacement C2 after failure. New campaign evidence supports dead-drop resolution and fallback; the IPNS protocol itself remains legitimate.
